Hi! I'm Pietro F. Maggi. I'm a father, a husband, and an engineer who still gets
excited about taking things apart to see how they work (I know, I can't really
blame my kids when they do the same).

If I have to define myself in a single phrase, I'm an *Extrovert Developer*.

## How I got here

My passion for technology has always started from the same place: solving real
problems, especially where software meets physical hardware. Over the years,
that curiosity took me across the stack, from bare-metal embedded systems and
RF/802.11 networking to backend services and mobile devices.

Early on, I found myself drawn to enterprise mobility: building practical
software for dedicated devices that people actually rely on in the field, back
when deploying a mobile solution meant flashing Windows Mobile "golden images"
onto rugged hardware. Later, working with enterprise mobile platforms and rugged
Android devices across EMEA (at Motorola Solutions and Zebra Technologies), I
discovered the part of engineering I enjoy the most: digging into real-world
problems, reading the source code when the docs fall short, and helping other
developers make sense of complex systems.

That mix of hands-on mobile engineering and genuinely enjoying conversations
with developers naturally pulled me into Developer Relations and public speaking
at community events like Droidcon and the Android Developer Summit.

Eventually, all those threads came together when I joined the **Android
Enterprise** team at **Google in Zurich**. Looking back, it feels like a very
natural step: bringing together years of embedded and enterprise mobile
experience to help shape how Android works for businesses and developers at
scale.

## Outside the day job

Whenever I find the time, I'm still tinkering: reviving old ThinkPads with
Linux, wiring up a Raspberry Pi to an eInk display, experimenting with an
Arduino, or building small web utilities (like [a client-side PDF
merger](/projects/adf-pdf-merger/) for my single-sided scanner) to scratch an
everyday itch.

This site is where I collect those notes, [public talks](/pages/talks/), and
small computing [projects](/projects/).
